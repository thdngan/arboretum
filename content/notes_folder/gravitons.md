A graviton would be the quantum of the gravitational field, massless and spin 2. Neither number is a guess. Massless gives a force of infinite range, which matches what [[gravity]] does. Spin 2 follows from the source being the stress-energy tensor, which carries two indices where the electromagnetic current carries one. A spin-2 field is also the only one that couples to energy universally, which is why gravity pulls on everything rather than on some particular charge.

Quantising the linearised Einstein equations produces a graviton without much difficulty. The trouble starts one loop up. General relativity is non-renormalisable, so keeping the perturbation series finite needs an unbounded number of independent parameters, and the theory stops predicting anything at around the Planck energy. What replaces it up there is the open question, and string theory and loop quantum gravity are two attempts at an answer.

Individual gravitons are likely undetectable in principle and not merely in practice. The cross-section is small enough that a detector built to catch one graviton from any plausible source would need so much mass that it would collapse before it could run. LIGO measures gravitational waves, which are the classical field, and says nothing about whether that field is quantised, in the same way that a radio antenna does not demonstrate [[photons]].
