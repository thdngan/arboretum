As one of the 4 fundamental forces of nature, the strong interaction is the most powerful, easily overpowering gravity and electromagnetism within its tiny domain. It is basically what binds [[quarks]] together to form [[protons]], [[neutrons]], and other [[hadrons]], and it does that through [[gluons]], the force-carrying [[bosons]] that shuttle colour charge between [[quarks]].

The strange part is that it does not fall off with distance the way the other forces do. Pull 2 quarks apart and the field between them collapses into a narrow tube whose energy climbs steadily with separation, so the force between them stays roughly constant however far you go and the stored energy keeps mounting. Sooner or later there is enough of it to make a fresh quark-antiquark pair, which costs less than stretching any further, and what you are left with is 2 [[hadrons]] rather than 2 free quarks, which is confinement, and it is why nobody has ever seen an isolated quark.

The same coupling runs the other way at short range. [[quarks|Quarks]] sitting close together inside a [[protons|proton]] or [[neutrons|neutron]] behave almost as though they were free, which is asymptotic freedom (it earned a Nobel Prize and it is the reason quantum chromodynamics (QCD) can be calculated at all at high energies).

There is also a leftover. The colour force does not quite cancel outside a hadron, and what leaks out is the residual strong force, which binds [[protons]] and [[neutrons]] into nuclei and overcomes the intense repulsion between positively charged [[protons]]. It is far weaker than the force inside a nucleon and dies off fast --> why there is an upper limit on how large a nucleus can get.
