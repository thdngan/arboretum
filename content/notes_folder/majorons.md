A majoron is a hypothetical [[bosons|boson]] that appears if lepton number is a global symmetry and that symmetry breaks spontaneously rather than holding exactly. Breaking a continuous global symmetry leaves a massless Goldstone boson behind, and the majoron is that boson for lepton number.

The motivation is [[neutrinos|neutrino]] mass. The seesaw mechanism explains why neutrinos weigh so little by pairing them with something very heavy, but it requires a neutrino to be its own antiparticle, which violates lepton number. If that violation comes from a broken symmetry rather than being written in by hand, a majoron comes with it.

It would couple to neutrinos and to almost nothing else, so looking for one directly is not realistic. The handle is neutrinoless double beta decay. Without a majoron, all the released energy goes into two [[electrons]] and the summed electron spectrum has a sharp line at the endpoint. With one, the majoron carries off part of that energy and the line becomes a broad continuum. Experiments search for both shapes.
