The process where atoms, molecules, or ions split apart, transforming into new, often more reactive forms.

In chemistry, dissociation typically refers to when a compound separates into smaller components, usually in solution. A classic example is ionic dissociation, where salts like sodium chloride (NaCl) dissolve in water, splitting into Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions. Acids and bases also rely on dissociation: hydrochloric acid (HCl) in water dissociates into H⁺ and Cl⁻, a fundamental process behind pH chemistry and biological functions.

In physics, dissociation often involves molecular bonds breaking due to energy input (from [[heat]], light, or electrical forces). In high-energy environments like plasmas, flames, or the upper atmosphere, molecules such as O₂ (oxygen) or H₂ (hydrogen) dissociate into individual atoms, which then feed reactions like combustion or ozone formation. In photodissociation, photons (light particles) provide the energy to break molecular bonds (this is how sunlight drives reactions in Earth’s atmosphere, creating and destroying ozone).
