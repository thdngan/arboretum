Dissociation is a molecule or an ionic solid coming apart into smaller pieces, usually reversibly.

In solution it is mostly about [[ions]]. NaCl separates into Na⁺ and Cl⁻ because water molecules surround each ion, and the energy released doing that pays for pulling the lattice apart. Acids do the same with a proton, and how far HA goes to H⁺ and A⁻ is what the dissociation constant $K_a$ measures. Strong acids dissociate essentially completely, weak ones settle at an equilibrium, and pH is a statement about where that equilibrium landed.

In the gas phase, breaking a bond takes energy, and the source can be [[heat]] or light. Photodissociation is the second case, where a [[photons|photon]] carrying more than the bond energy breaks the molecule apart. This runs the chemistry of the upper atmosphere. O₂ absorbing ultraviolet below about 240 nm splits into two oxygen atoms, each of which can attach to another O₂ to make ozone, and ozone photodissociates in turn at longer wavelengths. The ozone layer is the steady state between those two.
