Albedo is the fraction of incident light a surface reflects rather than absorbs, running from 0 for a perfect absorber to 1 for a perfect mirror. Fresh snow is about 0.8, open ocean about 0.06, and Earth as a whole about 0.3.

It enters the energy balance directly, since absorbed solar power goes as $(1-\alpha)$. The equilibrium temperature of a planet depends on its albedo as much as on how far it sits from its star. See [[naked planet climate model]].

The ice-albedo feedback is why it matters for climate. Ice reflects, so ice keeps things cold, so ice stays. Melt some and you expose ocean or land with a much lower albedo, more sunlight gets absorbed, and more ice melts. It runs the same way in both directions, which makes it a positive feedback whichever way it is going, and it is part of why the poles are warming faster than everywhere else.

Elsewhere in the solar system it is one of the first things you can measure and an awkward one to interpret. Venus reflects about 0.77 off its cloud deck, which is a large part of why it outshines every other planet in our sky. The Moon reflects about 0.12, roughly what worn asphalt does, and only looks bright because it is set against a black sky.
