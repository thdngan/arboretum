A neutron carries no net charge and is a [[baryons|baryon]] of one up [[quarks|quark]] and two down. At 939.6 MeV it is slightly heavier than a [[protons|proton]], which is what lets it decay into one. Nuclei need them because the [[strong interactions|strong force]] between nucleons does not care about charge while the electrostatic repulsion between protons does, so adding neutrons buys binding without adding repulsion.

Inside a nucleus a neutron can last indefinitely. On its own it lasts about 15 minutes before beta decay turns it into a proton, an [[electrons|electron]] and an antineutrino. The exact figure is unsettled (?).

It is neutral overall but not neutral inside. The quarks carry charge, so a neutron has a magnetic moment, and that is what lets neutron scattering probe magnetic order in solids.
