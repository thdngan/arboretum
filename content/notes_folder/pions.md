Pions are the lightest [[hadrons]], a [[quarks|quark]] and an antiquark drawn from the up and down flavours. There are three: π⁺ and π⁻ at 139.6 MeV, and π⁰ at 135 MeV.

They are light for a reason unrelated to the quark masses being small. Pions are the near-Goldstone bosons of the chiral symmetry that QCD breaks spontaneously, and their mass would be exactly zero if the up and down quarks were massless. Everything else built from quarks weighs at least as much as a [[protons|proton]].

The charged ones live 26 ns and decay to a [[muons|muon]] and a [[neutrinos|neutrino]], which is where atmospheric muons come from. The neutral one decays to two [[photons]] in 8.5×10⁻¹⁷ s, too fast to travel any measurable distance.

At nuclear scales the force between [[protons]] and [[neutrons]] can be written as pion exchange, and the 140 MeV mass puts the range at about 1.4 fm. This was the original model of the nuclear force and it predates any knowledge of [[quarks]], but it survives as an effective description because at those distances the [[gluons]] are not resolvable anyway.
