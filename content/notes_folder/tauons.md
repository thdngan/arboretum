A tauon is the third charged [[leptons|lepton]], same charge and spin as an [[electrons|electron]] and 3,477 times the mass, at 1,777 MeV. It lasts 2.9×10⁻¹³ s.

The mass is what separates it from the other two. A [[muons|muon]] at 106 MeV is too light to decay into anything containing [[quarks]], so it only ever produces an electron and [[neutrinos]]. A tauon outweighs both the [[pions|pion]] and the [[kaons|kaon]], so about two thirds of its decays are hadronic. That makes it the one lepton usable for studying the [[strong interactions|strong force]], and its hadronic decay rate is one of the cleaner ways to extract the strong coupling constant.

Each generation of lepton comes with its own neutrino, and the tau neutrino was the last of the three to be seen directly, in 2000. Producing one takes enough energy to make a tauon, and no natural source makes many.
