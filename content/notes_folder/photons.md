A photon is the gauge [[bosons|boson]] of the electromagnetic field, spin 1 and no rest mass. Its energy is $E = h\nu$, so the only thing separating a radio photon from a gamma ray is frequency.

Masslessness is why it moves at $c$ and why electromagnetism has infinite range, and both of those are testable. Experiments put the photon mass below about 10⁻¹⁸ eV. Any nonzero value would give a speed of light that depended on frequency and a Coulomb force that died off exponentially at long range.

A photon carries no electric charge, so photons pass through each other without interacting except through loops. [[gluons]] do not have this property, and that one difference is most of why quantum electrodynamics can be calculated on paper and quantum chromodynamics cannot.

The wave and particle descriptions are each incomplete. A photon arrives at a detector as one discrete event of energy $h\nu$, and where those events land is set by a wave amplitude. The interference pattern still builds up when the source is dim enough that only one photon is in the apparatus at a time.
