A blackbody absorbs every photon that lands on it, at every wavelength and every angle. The name is about absorption and not about appearance: a blackbody at 5,800 K is white.

It emits as well. Kirchhoff's law says a good absorber at some wavelength is an equally good emitter at that wavelength, so a perfect absorber emits as much as anything at that temperature can. What comes out depends only on temperature and not at all on what the object is made of, which is what makes the idealisation worth having.

Two consequences get used constantly. The spectrum peaks at

$$
\lambda_{peak} = \dfrac{b}{T}
$$

with $b \approx 2.9\times10^{-3}$ m·K, so hotter means bluer, and the total power emitted per unit area is $\sigma T^4$ ([[Stefan-Boltzmann law]]). Both drop out of Planck's law, which was the first calculation that could not be made to come out right without quantising energy.

Nothing real is a perfect blackbody. Stars and planets are close enough for most purposes, and the Sun's spectrum matches a 5,772 K blackbody well apart from absorption lines. Earth is a worse fit. Its emission is roughly that of a 255 K blackbody, but the atmosphere absorbs strongly in some infrared bands and hardly at all in others, and that wavelength dependence is the greenhouse effect.
