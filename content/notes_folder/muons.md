A muon is a [[leptons|lepton]] with the same charge and spin as an [[electrons|electron]] and 207 times the mass. The extra mass gives it somewhere to decay to, so it lasts 2.2 µs and then becomes an electron and two [[neutrinos]].

Cosmic rays hitting the upper atmosphere make them constantly, and about one crosses every square centimetre of ground per minute. In 2.2 µs a muon moving at nearly $c$ should cover 660 m, nowhere near the 15 km from where it forms down to the ground. They arrive anyway, because the Lorentz factor at those energies is a few tens and the proper time elapsed is shorter by that factor. It was one of the first measurements of time dilation on something behaving as a real clock.

They are heavy enough to get through metres of rock. Muon tomography measures how many are absorbed passing through a large object and turns that into a density map, which is how the void above the Grand Gallery in the Great Pyramid was found.
