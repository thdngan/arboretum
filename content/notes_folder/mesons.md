A meson is a [[quarks|quark]] and an antiquark bound by the [[strong interactions|strong force]]. Two half-integer spins sum to an integer, so mesons count as [[bosons]] despite being built out of [[fermions]]. None of them are stable, since a quark and an antiquark in the same object will eventually annihilate.

Before QCD, the force between [[protons]] and [[neutrons]] was modelled as an exchange of [[pions]], and that picture still works at nuclear distances even though the real carriers are [[gluons]]. The range follows from the pion mass: a 140 MeV exchange particle gives a force that dies off past about 1.4 fm, which is roughly the size of a nucleus.

[[pions|Pions]] are the lightest at 140 MeV and high-energy collisions produce more of them than anything else. [[kaons]] carry a strange quark, last long enough to be studied properly, and are where CP violation first showed up. Heavier states pairing a charm or bottom quark with its own antiquark, the charmonium and bottomonium families, move slowly enough inside themselves to be modelled with a simple potential, which makes them a clean way to measure what the strong force does as a function of distance.
