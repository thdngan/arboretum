A slurry is a messy hybrid of solid and liquid, where fine particles are suspended in a fluid, creating something that’s neither completely solid nor fully free-flowing. What makes a slurry work is its viscosity and stability. Too thin, and the solid particles settle out, forming a useless sludge at the bottom. If it's too thick, it won’t flow properly, and would clog up pipes and machines. In construction: concrete, drilling fluids, etc. In mining and metallurgy, they transport valuable minerals in water-based solutions, making it easier to process ore. Paper production relies on slurries of pulp and water, while agriculture uses slurries in fertilizers and animal feed processing.
