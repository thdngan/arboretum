The total power radiated per unit area by a [[blackbody]] depends on nothing but its temperature:

$$
P = \sigma T^4
$$

where $\sigma = 5.67\times10^{-8}$ W m⁻² K⁻⁴. For anything that is not a perfect blackbody, multiply by an emissivity between 0 and 1.

The fourth power is what makes this useful. A 1% change in temperature is a 4% change in emitted power, so radiative balance is stiff: a planet that absorbs a little more energy does not have to warm by much before it is radiating the excess straight back out. Run it backwards and the effective temperature of a planet follows from its distance and its [[albedo]] alone, which for Earth gives 255 K. The surface is about 288 K, and that 33 K gap is the greenhouse effect.

The same $T^4$ makes a star's luminosity $4\pi R^2 \sigma T^4$. Colour gives temperature, temperature and luminosity together give the radius, and none of it requires resolving the disc.
